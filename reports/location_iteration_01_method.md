# Location iteration 01: method and interpretation

This is the first complete inferred iteration of the four-value model, not an accepted historical balance. It deliberately preserves regional failures instead of setting capacities from population.

## Inputs owned here

`evidence/location_input_manifest.json` pins the imported source pack under `data/raw/location_inputs`. The importer accepts explicit paths and copies data; no sibling code or capacity-model runtime is called by `ha1300 locations`. Source origins in the manifest are historical provenance, not live dependencies. Raw game files and source data are ignored and are not redistributed.

The current reconstruction's native-grid crop scenarios are frozen intermediate evidence, generated by this repository's existing acquisition/calibration pipeline. Their actual bytes, source manifests, conversion configurations and code are bound to the location manifest. The underlying native-grid products and documented crop availability are retained; old per-location capacity assignments are not used. No population raster is read. Cached EU5 starting population is joined only after calculating the four values.

## Grid calculation

1. Retain representative historical crops. On non-cropping cells, record the first viable crop in the existing ecoregion's historical candidate list as a potential expansion analogue. No crop is chosen solely by the highest calories. Absence of a compatible candidate means no agricultural expansion under this crop scenario, not missing output.
2. Compute rainfed and irrigated annual food density from the calibrated low/high scenarios and shared historical management positions. Apply the existing processing, seed, losses, rotation and harvest conventions once. Irrigation inferior to rainfed is explicitly counted and not selected; its scenario is not silently relabelled as superior.
3. Extend the existing FORGE terrestrial-energy transfer to the uncultivated baseline of crop-labelled land. Retain existing pastoral estimates where that livelihood is assigned. These are modern-environment process transfers, not observed medieval densities. Aquatic food is excluded throughout this first iteration.
4. Reconstruct current cultivated and irrigated fractions from the dated HYDE slice. LUH2 supplies crop fraction where HYDE is missing; missing irrigation receives a nearest valid irrigated/cropped ratio analogue. HYDE and LUH remain one related evidence family. Missing numeric food values receive documented same-crop or same-livelihood spatial estimates.
5. Use SAGE potential vegetation as the vegetation-barrier classification. Baseline access fractions range from 0 for polar rock/ice to 15% for grassland; maximum rainfed fractions range from 0 to 70%. Both receive an ETOPO relief attenuation. **These fractions are explicit first-iteration shared priors, not dataset measurements.** Existing reconstructed cultivation is retained even when a coarse terrain prior is more pessimistic. No claim is made that YXX best-class yield implies a measured whole-cell suitable fraction.
6. Recompute a seasonal surface-water allocation using HydroBASINS, imported GRUN runoff and RiverATLAS transmission constraints. Six highest-reference-ET months are a first-pass crop-season analogue. Demand includes reference shortage, a 100 mm/season floor and, for wet rice, an additional 60 mm per active month. The paddy term is a conservative scenario assumption for field-water losses, not a medieval measured constant. These terms avoid treating humid rice as requiring no managed supply; they do not yet capture all drainage and flood-protection benefits.
7. Existing demand has priority. Expansion receives only remaining routed supply after a 60% runoff reserve and transmission loss, with 40% delivery efficiency. The least-supplied active month limits reliably served hectares; seasonal storage is absent. Save monthly source, withdrawal, loss and outflow accounts. This is a simultaneous upstream-first scenario, not independent per-location maximization of the same river water.
8. Clearing consumes land and displaces the prior livelihood. No gain is granted where crop support is inferior to the displaced livelihood. Water control adds the incremental benefit over the supported dry alternative; current and maximum cropped/served shares remain bounded by physical land.

## Maximum interpretation

The maximum keeps the historically assigned crop and rotation system fixed while expanding clearing and low-lift surface irrigation within the configured resource scenario. It includes existing improvements. It is not the technological maximum for every date in the game, and it does not claim exhaustive coverage of wells, qanats, seasonal reservoirs, flood regulation, drainage or land engineering. Existing crop-system effectiveness absorbs some historical water-control benefits without identifying them separately. This attribution remains a limitation for the later building pass.

## Final location aggregation and normalization

The game map registration is separable: linear longitude and cubic latitude. Transform every game-pixel edge and calculate exact spherical-area intersections with 5-arc-minute cells. The overlap matrix has 1,853,769 location/cell intersections for 20,929 land-model locations; numerical area conservation is checked. Registration itself is approximate, distinct from numerical overlap precision.

Where game coastlines cross raster ocean or tiny islands lack source-grid representation, use the nearest terrestrial field values and report the transferred area share. This is an explicit spatial estimate; it does not make the real and game coastlines identical. Such transfer cannot establish local historical accuracy.

Aggregate baseline, starting and maximum support first. Choose a positive full multiplier from area-weighted reference food density, with a floor of 0.01. Convert support to effective cropland by division. This preserves total support exactly and avoids the false result obtained by independently averaging multipliers and summing effective hectares.

`B = baseline_support / M`

`I_start = (starting_support - baseline_support) / M`

`I_max = (maximum_support - baseline_support) / M`

The normalized units are not physical hectares. Their scale remains a convention pending fixed building-package calibration.

## Complete map scope

All 28,573 template-backed game-map colours receive records and all four primary values. The 20,929 inherited land-model locations receive the physical calculation. Another 7,644 zones are explicitly classified from `default.map`; they receive zero settlement-capacity contributions and neutral multiplier 1 because they are non-settlement zones. This is a game-domain rule, not a claim that lakes or wastelands have no food resources. The 36 land-model locations also present in special non-ownable/wasteland lists retain their estimates, with eligibility exposed for later integration.

The native RGB location-ID raster is retained. Overview images may be downsampled for display; location records and final numerical values are never subsampled. The map search exposes all records.

## Uncertainty and completion

Shared yield sensitivity is evaluated at 75%, 100% and 125% of the central scenario. Combined +/-30% land and +/-25% yield brackets are reported as scenario ranges, not statistical confidence intervals. These are a first sensitivity pass, not independent historical validation.

Population shortfalls, high frontier support and zero terrestrial estimates remain visible. A zero terrestrial estimate is explicitly labelled and is not treated as missing data. It does not establish that a historically inhabited location could not survive through excluded food sources, different crops or missing water systems.

Completion requires all four values, the full inventory/map join, finite and ordered values, preserved support identities, monthly water conservation and a matching source/configuration/code/output fingerprint. Historical accuracy is reported separately.

## Reproduction

From this repository, with the imported source pack and existing supporting grid products present:

```bash
uv run ha1300 locations
uv run python scripts/validate_locations.py
uv run pytest -q
```

To regenerate supporting products from their owned inputs first:

```bash
uv run ha1300 acquire
uv run ha1300 run
uv run python scripts/acquire_water.py
uv run ha1300 water
uv run ha1300 locations
uv run python scripts/validate_locations.py
```

For a fresh private checkout, run `uv sync --group dev` and use `scripts/import_location_inputs.py --help` to import the licensed game geometry and the pinned historical source pack from an available archive/cache. The CLI never silently discovers another project or deploys to the game.


## Equal-area comparison

The viewer also offers a final game-unit comparison, initially selected, that removes
absolute location size. For modelled land, the common reference area is
sum(starting capacity) / sum(starting capacity / physical hectares).
Multiply base and all improvement amounts, and their derived capacities, by
reference area / physical location area. Keep the productivity multiplier unchanged.
This preserves global starting support and each location's starting/maximum ratio;
it does not preserve the global maximum. Starting population never enters this normalization.

Physical hectares, source fractions and basin water allocations remain those of the
original reconstruction. The comparison is not a new physical production estimate.
A region with more location subdivisions now receives more game support at equal
environmental conditions. Both versions use shared map colour scales. Nonsettlement
zones retain zero capacity. Full comparison values are in locations_equal_area.csv;
the four primary values are in location_values_equal_area.csv, and normalization
metadata in area_comparison.json. Reproduce both with uv run ha1300 locations.


## Settlement eligibility and usable-value gate

Eligibility is derived independently from the imported vanilla default.map:
template locations outside sea_zones, lakes, impassable_mountains and non_ownable
are is_ownable=true. Starting ownership and population are not eligibility filters.
The imported map definitions were checked byte-for-byte against the configured
installed game on 2026-09-12. No game files were modified.

settlement_validation.json and unresolved_settlements.csv distinguish row coverage
from usable settlement support. Every ownable location must have finite, ordered
primary values, positive multiplier and physical area, correct RGB and land/ownability
classification, and positive starting and maximum support to pass the readiness gate.
A finite zero placeholder is explicitly unresolved; this check never invents food or
uses a population floor. The initial eligibility audit found 20,893 ownable locations, all present,
but 132 zero-support cases (120 populated). The repair described below supersedes
that failed readiness result. The 36 modelled non-ownable
corridors retain physical estimates with is_ownable=false and display as excluded.

The native and overview maps both include every ownable location. The 93 zones absent
from the downsampled overview are all non-ownable; native geometry retains them.
Run uv run python scripts/validate_locations.py to evaluate the actual generated
physical-area and equal-area ledgers. It exits nonzero while settlement support is
unresolved, even when all structural tests and pytest regressions pass.


## Zero-support repair — 2026-09-13

Equal area is the working game version. The area-based comparison is shelved;
fine-grid physical accounting is still needed before final normalization.

The coarse FORGE-derived terrestrial model and its transfer can return zero support
for small islands and cold/arid settings. These are model outputs, not field observations
establishing that a game-ownable landscape has no terrestrial food. The repair treats
wholly unsupported ownable locations as explicit estimation gaps. It does not change
positive estimates simply because their population exceeds capacity.

Before aggregation, identify the source grid cells serving those locations under
the existing coastline registration. Estimate terrestrial support using eight nearby
environmental analogues, prioritizing the same ecoregion and always separating pastoral
and foraging donor pools. Similarity combines spherical distance (1,000 km scale),
temperature (5 C scale), log rainfall and log seasonality (unit scales). Where island
climate is absent, record a nearest same-livelihood climate donor. The lowest decile of
positive donor values is excluded to avoid near-zero interpolation tails, and the
replacement is the lower quartile of the selected donor estimates. These are shared
explicit modelling choices, not published historical coefficients.

No fixed population minimum is applied. Donor estimates scale with the existing food
model. Population is joined only after this calculation. No fish, cultivation,
irrigation service or extra water is added. Baseline, current and maximum food support
are recomputed together, preserving resource accounting and the four-value identities.
Replacement source cells, donor IDs, donor values, distances and missing-climate status
are saved in terrestrial_completion.json. terrestrial_analogue_share marks affected
locations; repaired_settlements.csv lists the former zero cases. Estimates remain
low-confidence, especially on isolated islands and in highly seasonal environments.

The completed run replaces 5,062 source cells serving the 132 zero-support cases.
All 20,893 ownable locations and all 3,819 ownable provinces have positive starting and
maximum support. Physical cropland fractions, irrigation service and water-account
artifacts are byte-identical to the prior run. Total physical starting support changes
by about 61,418 people (0.0082%); this is a completion repair, not global rebalance.
The strict delivery validator passes. New unit tests and a generated-data regression
check zero cases, donor provenance, eligibility, both output variants and provinces.
